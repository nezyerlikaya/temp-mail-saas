# v1.1 Seat Governance Specification

## Purpose

This document specifies future seat governance for enterprise customers. It does not implement seats, memberships, organizations, or billing behavior.

## Seat Types

Future seat types should include active seat, reserved seat, pending seat, suspended seat, archived seat, and service seat.

Active seats represent assigned users. Reserved seats represent planned capacity. Pending seats represent invitations or approval waits. Suspended seats preserve governance evidence while disabling access. Archived seats preserve historical billing context. Service seats represent future non-human or automation identities.

## Seat Lifecycle

Recommended lifecycle states are requested, approved, assigned, activated, suspended, reclaimed, and archived.

## Lifecycle Controls

Seat transitions should eventually require ownership rules, approval context, audit evidence, cost impact review, and access impact review. Reclamation should connect to joiner, mover, and leaver governance.

## Governance Risks

The most important seat risks are over allocation, under allocation, stale seats, service-seat misuse, suspended-seat leakage, and unclear ownership.

## Future Reporting

Future seat reporting should include assigned seats, reserved seats, inactive seats, suspended seats, service seats, reclamation candidates, and department or workspace allocation.

## Non-Implementation Boundary

STEP74 does not add seat tables, seat assignment logic, seat-based billing, or organization membership behavior.
